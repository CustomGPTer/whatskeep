Privacy policy

Last updated 26 November 2026. This policy covers the WhatsKeep app for Android (package com.jonjackson.whatskeep) and this website. WhatsKeep is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who can be reached by opening an issue on the project's GitHub page.

The short version

  • WhatsKeep saves notifications from the apps you choose, on your phone, encrypted.
  • It has no internet permission. There are no accounts, no servers, no advertising and no analytics. The developer receives nothing.
  • Saved captures delete themselves after the time you choose.

What stays on your phone

For each notification from an app you have chosen, WhatsKeep keeps the app's name, the title, the sender, the conversation name, the text, the time and any picture preview shown. Text, names and pictures are encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key held in Android's Keystore before they are written to the app's private storage. Captures are deleted after 30 days unless you choose a different period, and the archive is kept under a size limit you set. Android's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for WhatsKeep.

WhatsKeep has no internet, location, contacts, camera, microphone or storage permission.

Exports and backups

Text and picture exports are only made when you ask, and saved where you choose; they are not encrypted, and WhatsKeep does not delete them. Backups are encrypted with a password you set, which cannot be recovered.

Other people's messages

Notifications contain messages from other people. WhatsKeep keeps them only on your phone. Please treat them with care and use the app within the law.

Keeping and deleting your data

Delete one capture from its page, or everything in Settings, Retention and storage. Uninstalling WhatsKeep or clearing its data deletes the archive and its key.

Your rights

Because the developer holds no data about you, there is nothing to request, correct or erase on a server. Everything is already in your hands.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages. It uses no cookies and no analytics. The only script on it unlocks the download once the lawful-use box is ticked. GitHub may keep standard web server logs, such as IP addresses, under GitHub's privacy statement.
